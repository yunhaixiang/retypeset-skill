# Style Guide

## General Principles

1. Be faithful to the material. Respect the source and do not change the content of the material (unless deliberatedly asked by the user to make factual or stylistic changes), even when you think they are factually incorrect. When source is too illegible, mark it and report. 
2. Texify every math notation, and modernize notation where appropriate. Use the standard modern convention rather than merely changing typography, and do not make too drastic changes such as changing a variable `$a$` to `$A$`. For example, use `$\mathbb{F}_q$` for finite fields instead of `$F_q$`, write `\(\mathbb{G}_m\)` for the multiplicative group, not `\(G_m\)`, use \mathfrak for Lie algebras, \mathcal for sheaves, write base change as `\(G_{K'}\)`, not `\(G_{/K'}\)`, write Weil restriction as `\(\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m,K}\)`, not `\(R_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{G}_{m,K})\)`, and well known operators such as $\mathrm{Gal}$ or $\sin$ should be upright when it is usually written upright in modern text. 
3. Be consistent throughout the book. Use one convention and style of notation everywhere once chosen. For example, use `$L$`-functions consistently, not a mix of `$\mathrm{L}$`-functions and `$L$`-functions. 
4. Must ensure things are hyperlinked. Internal references to sections, chapters, appendices, equations, theorem-style environments, exercises, bibliography items, and comparable targets should be clickable, not left as plain text.
5. Do not use OCR in any way, instead use direct visual reasoning. Do not generate, consult, search, or rely on OCR text for transcription, lookup, navigation, verification, or cross-checking. The authoritative source is the complete PDF/page image inspected visually; when in doubt, re-open the full page image and read it directly.

## Specific Conventions

1. Use `$\ell$`-adic, not `l-adic` or `$l$`-adic.
3. Respect diacritics in author names, including named theorems and criteria. If an author's name has diacritics, preserve them in text, headings, table-of-contents entries, lookup metadata, and bibliography entries. For example, write `Čebotarev`, `Šafarevič`, and `Néron`, not `Cebotarev`, `Safarevic`, or `Neron`.
4. Use proper mathematical symbols in LaTeX and metadata, not programming-style ASCII operators. For example, write `\(\geq\)`, `\(\ne\)`, and `\(\to\)`, not `>=`, `!=`, or `->`.
5. TeXify mathematical notation wherever it appears, including headings, table-of-contents strings, bibliography data, and lookup metadata. For example, write `\(\chi_E\)`, `\(G_\ell\)`, and `\(S_m\)`, not `chi_E`, `G_ell`, or `Sm`.
5a. Write `\(p\)-curvature` with `p` in math mode everywhere it appears, including short titles used for the table of contents and running heads. Use `\texorpdfstring{\(p\)}{p}` when needed in moving arguments or PDF strings.
6. When joining authors' names in compound terms, use an en dash. In LaTeX source, write `Hodge--Tate`, not `Hodge-Tate`; similarly use `Néron--Ogg--Šafarevič` and comparable author-name compounds.
6a. Use the correct diacritic in standard mathematical terms. Write `étale`, not `etale`, in prose and bibliography text.
7. The book must be well-hyperlinked. Add stable `\label{...}` commands to sections, theorems, propositions, lemmas, corollaries, definitions, remarks, equations, appendices, and bibliography items when they are referenced. Use hyperlinked reference commands such as `\cref{...}` and `\Cref{...}` for internal references, and biblatex citation commands such as `\cite{...}` or `\autocite{...}` for bibliographic references; do not leave theorem, section, equation, appendix, or citation references as unlinked plain text. References to theorem-style environments must include the environment name and source-consistent number, e.g. write a clickable `\hyperref[lem:1.2.2]{Lemma~1.2.2}` or `\hyperref[prop:1.2.3]{Proposition~1.2.3}`, not plain `1.2.2` or `1.2.3`.
8. Keep the bibliography in the same bibliography style as the the source. 
9. Indent the first paragraph after chapter, section, subsection, and similar headings.
10. If a theorem-style mathematical environment begins with a list or an item, put the list on the next line after the environment heading. In LaTeX, insert `\leavevmode\par` immediately after `\begin{...}` and before `\begin{enumerate}` or the first `\item`, so the first item does not run in after handles such as ``Exercise'', ``Examples'', or ``Theorem''.
10a. Theorem-style headings should visually follow standard `amsthm` display: a bold heading such as `Lemma 2.6.14.` with a period after the number/name and the standard compact heading space before the statement. Do not use ad hoc wide gaps after theorem labels. If source-numbered theorem-like material cannot use a normal theorem environment, its shared macro must still reproduce this punctuation and spacing.
11. Always use upright `\mathrm{D}` for derived categories, e.g. write `\(\mathrm{D}^b_c(X,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\)`, not `\(D^b_c(X,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\)`.
12. Always use `\cong` for isomorphisms, not `\simeq`, `\sim`, or an arrow decorated with `\sim`.
13. Use parentheses `(...)` for parenthesized sentences and comments in prose, not square brackets `[...]`, unless the brackets are literally part of a citation label, bibliographic handle, optional LaTeX argument, or source mathematical notation.
14. Hyperlinks must be visibly colored in the compiled PDF. Keep `hyperref` configured with `colorlinks=true` and explicit non-black colors for `linkcolor`, `citecolor`, and `urlcolor`.
15. Keep the numbering system and all numbering consistent with the source. Theorem-style numbers, paragraph numbers, section numbers, bibliography handles, and table-of-contents entries should match the source numbering scheme; do not renumber automatically in a way that changes the source-visible numbers.
16. Numbered lists should use parenthesized labels such as `(1)`, `(2)`, `(3)`, not `1)`, `2)`, `3)`. In LaTeX, use `\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]`.
17. Consecutive displayed equations should be combined into one display using `aligned`, `alignedat`, `gathered`, or a comparable AMS alignment environment, rather than written as multiple adjacent `\[...\]` blocks.
18. Use modern Verdier dual notation. Prefer a dedicated operator such as `\(\mathbb{D}\)` (or the locally defined Verdier-dual macro, if one exists) for Verdier duality, and do not confuse it with `\(\mathrm{D}\)` for derived categories.
19. Right and left derived functor symbols should be upright. Write `\(\mathrm{R}f_*\)`, `\(\mathrm{R}f_!\)`, and `\(\mathrm{L}f^*\)`, not `\(Rf_*\)`, `\(Rf_!\)`, or `\(Lf^*\)`.
20. Vertical spacing should look deliberate and even. Use shared macros and preamble lengths for theorem-style headings, source-numbered paragraphs, proofs, displays, and lists; do not rely on accidental blank lines or ad hoc `\vspace`, `\smallskip`, `\medskip`, or `\bigskip` commands around individual blocks or math environments. Source-numbered paragraphs such as `(2.10.7)` should use the shared paragraph macro and have deliberate top spacing comparable to theorem-style source headings, including when they immediately follow a proof. Proof environments should use normal block separation both before `Proof.` and after `\end{proof}`, for consistency with surrounding theorem-style material and paragraphs. Consecutive block spacings must collapse to a single normal unit; use `\addvspace` in shared block macros rather than unconditional `\vspace`.
21. Use the section symbol whenever a section number appears, including section headings, table-of-contents entries, running heads, and references to sections. Prefer linked references such as `\cref{sec:...}` or explicit `\hyperref[sec:...]{\S2.6}` over plain `2.6`.
22. Use `tikzcd` for commutative diagrams, not `array`, `CD`, or hand-spaced arrow diagrams.
23. References to material in later, not-yet-transcribed chapters must still use the correct target type and source number. Do not replace a theorem, lemma, proposition, corollary, definition, paragraph, or equation reference by a nearby section reference merely because the target chapter is incomplete. Instead, create a stable placeholder anchor in the future chapter with the correct type and number, e.g. `\sourceheading[lem:5.9.3]{Lemma 5.9.3}`, and link to it as `\hyperref[lem:5.9.3]{Lemma~5.9.3}` until the full text is transcribed.
